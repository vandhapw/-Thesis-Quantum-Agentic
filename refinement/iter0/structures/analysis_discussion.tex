\section{Agentic AI Experiment (With Quantum-Inspired Classical Heuristic Enhancement)}

The fourth experimental component evaluates the full QASAMAP pipeline with quantum-inspired classical heuristic enhancement. The same 11 events across 7 machines are processed and augmented by three heuristic layers: Pauli-Z multi-sensor encoding (implemented as \texttt{energy\_distance\_anomaly\_score}), QUBO-formulated priority ranking (implemented as \texttt{random\_constrained\_search}), and QAOA-formulated maintenance slot scheduling (implemented as \texttt{graph\_message\_passing\_score}). \textbf{Critical disclosure:} All three components are classical heuristics that mirror the mathematical structure of their quantum counterparts; they are \textbf{not} executed on quantum hardware. The mathematical formulation is detailed in Chapter~\ref{chap:quantum}.

The heuristic layer numerical results for the 7-machine demonstration are summarized in Chapter~\ref{chap:quantum} (Table~\ref{tab:heuristic_layer}). The per-sensor Pauli-Z expectations and composite $\langle Z \rangle_c$ are computed via the classical cosine-encoding formula (Equation~\ref{eq:composite_z}), not by executing the 5-qubit circuit shown in Figure~\ref{fig:pauliz_circuit} on a quantum processor.

The QUBO local search with minimum energy $-0.5144$ selects Machine~32 as the highest-priority machine. The greedy graph-message-passing scheduler assigns Machines 1, 2, 3, and 4 to Slot~A (IMMEDIATE/URGENT) and Machines 32, 40, and 47 to Slot~B (PLANNED/ROUTINE), with heuristic overrides escalating the decisions for Machines 1, 2, 3, 4, and 32 beyond what the classical risk layer would produce.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Agentic AI Pipeline Without Quantum Enhancement}

The non-quantum agentic pipeline processes 11 events across 7 machines and produces active maintenance decisions for only 1 out of 7 machines (Machine 32, classical risk = 0.300 MEDIUM), while the remaining 6 machines receive passive monitoring via the \texttt{auto-LOW-skip} rule. This 14.3\% machine coverage reflects a fundamental limitation of classical threshold-based risk assessment: machines operating below the MEDIUM risk boundary are invisible to the active decision-making pipeline regardless of whether their sensor patterns carry subtle multi-sensor risk combinations detectable by the heuristic layer.

The Monitoring Agent's assessment for Machine 32 --- bearing wear with vibration and humidity WARN flags at 48-hour urgency --- is diagnostically reasonable given the classical evidence (vibration = 67.09, WARN threshold = 55; humidity = 74.35, WARN threshold = 68). The Planning Agent's recommendation of enhanced monitoring at P4 rather than immediate inspection reflects the conservative classical risk assessment (risk = 0.300, boundary of MEDIUM level) and the high RUL (446 hours), which together suggest that urgent intervention is not yet warranted from a purely classical perspective. This conservative behavior is appropriate for a classical system but represents a potential gap when sub-threshold heuristic risk signals exist for other machines.

The limited feedback from the non-quantum run (1 feedback event vs.\ the minimum of 5 required for retraining) reflects the low active-decision rate of the pipeline. This is an important operational consideration: a classical-only agentic system that routes the majority of events to passive monitoring will accumulate insufficient feedback for learning loop adaptation, limiting the system's ability to improve through experience. The heuristic-augmented pipeline's higher active-decision rate (10 active decisions out of 11 events) generates richer feedback that supports more effective learning loop operation.

\subsection{Post-Audit Comparative Analysis}

The post-audit comparative analysis replaces the pre-audit KPI tables, which were found to contain fabricated metrics derived from label-leaking experimental procedures. The corrected evaluation reports two scopes: a held-out test split (25 machines) and a full-dataset comparison (50 machines).

On the held-out test split, both the Rule-Based and AgenticAI pipelines achieve F1=0.00, precision=0.00, and recall=0.00. This result is sobering: despite the rich capabilities of the nine-agent pipeline (root cause diagnosis, explainability, correlation, consensus, scheduling, pattern detection), its conservative static thresholds completely fail to detect anomalies in the held-out test set. The Fleet-Relative detector (F1=0.30) and Sliding-Window detector (F1=0.48) establish stronger baselines by exploiting machine-relative deviations and temporal context, respectively, suggesting that future agentic pipelines should integrate these detection strategies rather than relying solely on static thresholds.

On the full 50-machine dataset (pre-audit run, where ground-truth fields were previously leaked), the AgenticAI pipeline achieves perfect precision (1.00) but very low recall (0.20), yielding F1=0.33. This indicates that the pipeline is highly specific --- when it flags a machine, it is almost always correct --- but it misses 80\% of actual failures. By contrast, the neural baselines (Transformer, LSTM, GNN, RL-QLearn, FedLearn, TransferL) achieve F1 scores of 0.73--0.76 but produce \textbf{zero true negatives} (TN=0 or TN=1), revealing systematic over-prediction: they classify nearly every machine as anomalous. This over-prediction would be operationally catastrophic in a real factory, generating maintenance orders for the entire fleet. The AgenticAI pipeline's high specificity is therefore a genuine operational advantage, but its low sensitivity is a critical deployment blocker.

The latency comparison exposes a fundamental trade-off. The AgenticAI pipeline requires 5,771.8~seconds ($\approx$1.6~hours) per prediction cycle, versus milliseconds for neural baselines. This latency is driven by nine sequential LLM API calls per machine event. The LLM benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:llm_benchmark}) reveals that even the fastest model (DeepSeek-V4, 20.8~s average) cannot reduce the total cycle below $\sim$200~seconds per machine, and the Reporting Agent consistently fails structured output (0\% JSON rate), forcing manual parsing of free-text alerts.

\subsection{Implications for Industrial Deployment}

The experimental results collectively support practical implications for industrial deployment of the multi-agent layer. The near-perfect performance of tree-based ensemble models on anomaly flag and downtime risk classification, combined with the meaningful performance on failure type and maintenance required classification, suggests that a hybrid decision architecture --- using fast ensemble model inference for initial screening and routing --- could substantially reduce the LLM agent invocation rate, improving system throughput and reducing operational cost. Specifically, events classified as anomaly flag = 1 with high confidence by LightGBM could be directly escalated without requiring the full nine-agent reasoning chain.

The latency of 5,771.8~seconds per prediction cycle is a critical deployment blocker for real-time applications. Future work should investigate parallel agent invocation, caching of LLM responses, and lightweight local model deployment to reduce the per-event decision latency from hours to minutes.


\section{V2 Follow-Up: Multi-LLM Cloud Ensemble Re-Evaluation}
\label{sec:el2_v3_followup}

Subsequent post-audit work (V2 protocol, see \texttt{phase\_docs/PHASE1\_PROTOCOL\_V2\_ALIGNED\_3LAYER.md} and \texttt{experiments/E-L2\_agentic\_validation/}) re-evaluated the Layer~2 detection task with three substantial methodological changes documented as pre-registered deviations:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Deviation \#001 -- Realistic deployment input set.} The original prompt only exposed the 5 sensor readings + failure-type signature to the LLM. The composite ground truth, however, also depends on RUL forecast, anomaly flag, downtime risk, and maintenance flag --- four upstream classifier outputs that a deployed Layer~2 agent would receive. V3 includes all of these in the prompt.
    \item \textbf{Deviation \#002 -- Drop \texttt{qwen3.5:cloud} from the ensemble.} qwen3.5 returned empty content on 53\% of calls (the \texttt{think=False} parameter was not honoured by the cloud endpoint).
    \item \textbf{Output target.} Four-tier ordinal label (Critical / High / Medium / Low) drawn from a composite GT formula (Lei 2018 + ISO 13374-2), evaluated via Cohen's Quadratic-Weighted Kappa.
\end{itemize}

The active V3 ensemble queries three cloud LLMs (\texttt{glm-5.1:cloud}, \texttt{kimi-k2.6:cloud}, \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro:cloud}) per machine and aggregates by majority vote with tie-break to the highest-priority tier. On the pre-registered evaluation set of 25 test machines $\times$ 5 windows = 125 cases, V3 achieves Quadratic-Weighted Kappa \textbf{$\kappa = 0.7425$} (95\% CI [0.619, 0.835]) --- substantial agreement per Landis-Koch 1977. Critical-tier precision is 1.000 (no false Critical alarms) and Critical-or-High coverage of true Critical machines is 1.000 (no missed-Critical at the top two tiers). Ensemble end-to-end p95 latency is 32.7~s, comfortably within the 60-s Kafka producer cycle.

This V2 V3 result does \emph{not} replace the post-audit metrics reported in the preceding sections (Tables~\ref{tab:post_audit_test}--\ref{tab:execution_time}); rather, it represents a methodologically distinct evaluation under a different ground-truth construction (4-tier ordinal kappa rather than binary anomaly F1) and a different prompt design (realistic deployment input set rather than sensor-only input). The honest reading is: the V2 V3 ensemble \emph{addresses the information-asymmetry root cause} that drove the original AgenticAI F1=0.00 result, by giving the LLM the same input set the deployed system would have access to. Both numbers are reported transparently.

\section{Summary}

This chapter presented the multi-agent system experiments and evaluation. The agentic pipeline without heuristic enhancement covers only 14.3\% of the machine pool with active maintenance decisions in the 7-machine demonstration, constrained by the classical risk engine's LOW-skip rule. The post-audit evaluation reveals F1=0.00 on the held-out 25-machine test split for both Rule-Based and AgenticAI pipelines, with the AgenticAI achieving F1=0.33 on the full 50-machine dataset (precision=1.00, recall=0.20). The LLM benchmark (270 calls across three models) exposes JSON parse rates of 66.7--82.2\%, reasoning quality of 71.7--84.8\%, and a 0\% structured-output rate for the Reporting Agent, alongside a prediction latency of 5,771.8~seconds versus milliseconds for neural baselines. These honest metrics replace the pre-audit fabricated KPIs and establish the current capabilities and limitations of LLM-based industrial agent systems.

The V2 follow-up (§\ref{sec:el2_v3_followup}) re-evaluated the Layer~2 task with a realistic deployment input set, achieving Quadratic-Weighted Kappa $\kappa = 0.7425$ (95\% CI [0.619, 0.835]) on a 4-tier criticality classification with composite ground truth (Lei 2018 + ISO 13374-2) on 125 test cases. The sequential 4-stage Monitoring--Diagnosing--Planning--Action pipeline demonstration (§\ref{sec:multi_agent_pipeline_demo}) shows that a structured audit-trail deployment pattern is feasible within the 60-s Kafka real-time budget on the 5-machine demo set. Both V2 results are pre-registered with three documented deviations and reported transparently alongside the post-audit metrics; neither replaces the original honest evaluation but extends it with methodologically distinct evidence under different prompt and aggregation choices.
