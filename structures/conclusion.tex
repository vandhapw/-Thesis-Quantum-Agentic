\subsection{Summary of Contributions}

This thesis has presented QASAMAP --- the Quantum-Agentic Smart Manufacturing Anomaly detection and Predictive Maintenance framework --- as a principled, integrated solution to the multidimensional challenge of intelligent maintenance decision support in modern industrial environments. The research was grounded in a 100,000-reading smart manufacturing dataset representing 50 machines monitored across 69 days, whose statistical properties --- an 8.92\% anomaly rate, five distinct failure modalities, 16,333 critical low-RUL readings, and temporal non-stationarity --- provided both the motivation and the empirical basis for every architectural decision in QASAMAP. Building from this empirical foundation, the thesis conducted systematic literature synthesis, gap analysis, architectural design, comprehensive experimental evaluation, and critical assessment of limitations.

The \textbf{first primary contribution} is the T-GCN spatiotemporal forecasting architecture with cross-correlation lag graph construction. By building a directed graph over 50 machines (50 nodes, 538 edges) from pairwise lagged cross-correlations of the temperature time series, and combining Graph Convolution layers for spatial aggregation with GRU layers for temporal modeling, the T-GCN achieves an average R\textsuperscript{2} of 0.7702 across five sensor channels. This represents a 12.4\% improvement over the OptimizedLSTM baseline (R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.6853) and an 8.0\% improvement over the Hybrid LSTM-GCN (R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.7134), demonstrating that graph-based cross-machine dependency modeling provides a meaningful and consistent benefit for industrial sensor stream forecasting.

The \textbf{second primary contribution} is a comprehensive empirical benchmark of five supervised learning algorithms (XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, HistGradientBoosting, TabNet) across six machine health prediction targets. Tree-based ensemble models achieve near-perfect performance on binary classification targets such as anomaly flag (best F1 = 0.999, LightGBM) and downtime risk (best F1 = 0.999, LightGBM), reflecting the high discriminability of these targets. More challenging tasks --- machine status (best F1 = 0.712, XGBoost), failure type (best F1 = 0.880, XGBoost), and maintenance required (best F1 = 0.872, XGBoost) --- provide meaningful performance benchmarks. The RUL regression results (best R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.189, CatBoost) confirm the fundamental difficulty of single-snapshot RUL prediction without temporal degradation trajectory context, motivating the use of the T-GCN architecture for this task.

The \textbf{third primary contribution} is the empirical validation of the Layer~2 agentic AI multi-tier detection ensemble (E-L2 V3, reported in Chapter~5). A 3-LLM cloud ensemble (\texttt{glm-5.1:cloud}, \texttt{kimi-k2.6:cloud}, \texttt{deepseek-v4-pro:cloud}) achieves Quadratic-Weighted Kappa $\kappa = 0.7425$ (95\% CI [0.619, 0.835]) against composite ground truth (Lei 2018 + ISO 13374-2) on 125 multi-window QASAMAP test cases. This is substantial agreement per Landis-Koch 1977, with both point estimate and lower CI bound exceeding the pre-registered $\kappa \geq 0.6$ threshold required to authorise downstream Layer~3 scheduling. Critical-tier precision is 1.000 (no false Critical alarms) and Critical-or-High coverage of true Critical machines is 1.000 (no missed Critical at the top two tiers). Ensemble end-to-end p95 latency is 32.7~s, well within the 60-s Kafka producer cycle for real-time deployment. The result is reported transparently with three pre-registered methodology deviations documented (V1 sensor-only $\kappa = 0.362$ STOP, V2 decision-tree-prompt $\kappa = 0.191$ STOP exposing an information-asymmetry methodological flaw, V3 realistic-deployment-input-set $\kappa = 0.742$ PASS) --- all intermediate results preserved in the repository for full transparency.

The \textbf{fourth primary contribution} is the empirical evaluation of the Layer~3 maintenance scheduling optimisation across classical and quantum-class solvers (E-L3A static + E-L3B dynamic, reported in Chapter~6). Across 315 controlled solver runs (3 problem sizes $\times$ 3 employee groups $\times$ 5 random seeds $\times$ 6 solvers with size-conditional skipping), all four classical heuristics (Greedy, Genetic Algorithm, Simulated Annealing, Tabu Search) converge to identical optimal makespan with 100\% feasibility and sub-second wall-clock at the largest tested scale (50 machines $\times$ 5 days $\times$ 20 employees = 45000 binary variables). The two evaluated quantum-class methods (simulated Quantum Annealing via dwave-neal Ising mode, and quantum-inspired Simulated Bifurcation per Goto et al.\ 2019) consistently fail to produce feasible solutions on the dense scheduling QUBO encoding (paired Wilcoxon signed-rank $p < 10^{-5}$ vs classical-best). Gate-model QAOA on Qiskit statevector simulator is not applicable at meaningful problem sizes due to the qubit budget ($\sim$18 qubits practical, versus $\geq$45 variables required at the smallest meaningful instance). All four classical solvers also pass the dynamic rolling-horizon real-time feasibility test (E-L3B: 135 re-optimisation events, p95 wall-clock $<$ 1.5~s vs 60-s budget). Combined with the Layer~2 ensemble latency, end-to-end Layer~2 + Layer~3 pipeline operates within the 60-s Kafka producer cycle by $\geq 26$ s margin. The honest empirical finding is that QASAMAP-scale maintenance scheduling is currently best-served by classical methods on simulator-only evaluation; no quantum-advantage claim is made. Real D-Wave hardware was sought but not available; the architectural-readiness positioning is anchored on industrial precedents (Ford Otosan 6$\times$, BASF 7200$\times$ on adjacent scheduling problems) and the Cerezo et al.\ 2025 skepticism literature is directly addressed in a dedicated reconciliation section.

\subsection{Research Objective Verification}

The five research objectives formulated in Chapter 1 have been addressed and the following conclusions are drawn.

\textbf{Objective 1} (T-GCN spatiotemporal forecasting) is fully achieved. The T-GCN architecture with cross-correlation lag graph construction, machine-wide propagation with anomaly injection, and real-time streaming with partial sensor imputation via \texttt{AnchorBufferManager} is implemented, evaluated, and demonstrated to outperform both LSTM and Hybrid LSTM-GCN baselines on the 100,000-reading dataset (avg.\ R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.7702 vs.\ 0.6853 and 0.7134).

\textbf{Objective 2} (Multi-target supervised classification and regression) is fully achieved. Five classification targets and one regression target are evaluated across five algorithms, providing complete performance benchmarks for all machine health prediction tasks defined in the dataset schema.

\textbf{Objective 3} (Quantum computing integration) is achieved at the architectural level with an explicit honest empirical finding. The Layer~3 scheduling pipeline integrates two quantum-class solvers (simulated Quantum Annealing, quantum-inspired Simulated Bifurcation) alongside four classical baselines (Greedy, GA, SA, TS), pre-registered and evaluated across 315 controlled instances (E-L3A) and 135 dynamic re-optimisation events (E-L3B). The honest empirical finding at simulator-only evaluation is that classical heuristics dominate quantum-class methods in both solution quality (100\% feasibility vs 0--20\% for quantum-class) and wall-clock time. Gate-model QAOA is not applicable at QASAMAP-realistic problem sizes due to the qubit budget. The exclusive use of simulators rather than physical quantum hardware is the primary caveat on this objective; real D-Wave Advantage hardware was sought but the Leap free tier was not available at runtime. Real-NISQ-hardware replication is reserved as future work, with industrial precedents from Ford Otosan (6$\times$ scheduling speedup) and BASF (7200$\times$ liquid-filling) cited as supporting evidence for the architectural-readiness positioning on adjacent scheduling problem classes.

\textbf{Objective 4} (Multi-agent agentic AI system) is achieved with the V3 substantial-agreement empirical result. The Layer~2 detection ensemble is pre-registered (E-L2 protocol) and evaluated through three documented methodology iterations: V1 sensor-only-prompt achieved $\kappa = 0.362$ (STOP), V2 decision-tree-prompt with qwen3.5 dropped achieved $\kappa = 0.191$ (STOP, exposing an information-asymmetry methodological flaw), and V3 realistic-deployment-input-set achieved $\kappa = 0.7425$ (PASS, substantial agreement). All intermediate results preserved transparently. Operational safety is established by perfect Critical-or-High coverage of true Critical machines (1.000 --- no missed critical) and perfect Critical-tier precision (1.000 --- no false critical alarms). Real-time feasibility is established by ensemble e2e p95 latency of 32.7~s within the 60-s Kafka cycle.

\textbf{Objective 5} (Comparative evaluation across system configurations) is achieved through the unified PHASE 1 V2 protocol document and per-experiment STATISTICAL\_REPORT.md files (E-L2 V1 / V2 / V3, E-L3A, E-L3B), with explicit what-can-be-claimed and what-cannot-be-claimed sections per experiment, three pre-registered methodology deviation documents (PRE\_REG\_DEVIATION\_001/002/003.md), and a defense-ready synthesis narrative that calibrates every claim to the evidence level actually achieved. The end-to-end Layer~2 + Layer~3 pipeline is empirically demonstrated to operate within the 60-s real-time Kafka producer cycle with $\geq 26$ s margin for any classical Layer~3 solver choice.

\subsection{Limitations and Critical Reflections}

Scientific integrity demands honest assessment of the limitations of this research alongside its contributions. The most significant limitation is the reliance on classical simulation of quantum circuits rather than execution on physical quantum hardware. All quantum computing results in this thesis are obtained from Qiskit Aer statevector simulations that assume perfect qubit fidelity, zero gate error rates, and idealized noise models. The transition to physical NISQ hardware will require addressing gate error rates, qubit decoherence, and limited connectivity that incur additional circuit overhead. The quantum computing results should therefore be interpreted as upper bounds on achievable quantum advantage under ideal conditions, pending validation on physical hardware.

The Layer~2 agentic AI evaluation (E-L2 V3) used 25 test machines $\times$ 5 windows = 125 evaluation cases, which is sufficient for statistical power on the primary metric (Quadratic-Weighted Kappa with 1000-bootstrap CI: lower bound 0.619 above the 0.6 threshold) but does not constitute a fleet-wide evaluation over all 50 machines. The Layer~3 scheduling evaluation (E-L3A + E-L3B) used 315 controlled solver runs and 135 dynamic re-optimisation events across 3 problem sizes (up to 50 machines $\times$ 5 days $\times$ 20 employees = 45000 binary variables), which is sufficient to characterise classical solver dominance and quantum-class infeasibility on the dense scheduling QUBO encoding tested but does not generalise to alternative QUBO encodings or larger problem instances. Future work should conduct fleet-wide evaluation, expert-annotation validation of the composite ground truth used in Layer~2, and exploration of alternative scheduling QUBO encodings that may be more amenable to quantum-class solvers.

The supervised classification benchmarks reveal that the anomaly flag and downtime risk targets are near-perfectly discriminable by all tested models (F1 $> 0.998$), which suggests these labels may be directly derivable from deterministic rules rather than representing a genuine learning challenge. The more practically meaningful classification targets are machine status, failure type, and maintenance required, where F1 scores in the range 0.71--0.88 reflect genuine learning difficulty. The RUL regression results (R\textsuperscript{2} $\leq 0.19$) across all models indicate that static feature-based RUL prediction is fundamentally limited, reinforcing the importance of the T-GCN temporal approach for this task.

The dataset, while large at 100,000 observations, represents a single industrial context and may not generalize universally across manufacturing environments, machine types, or failure mode distributions. Validation on publicly available benchmark datasets such as the NASA C-MAPSS turbofan degradation dataset, the PRONOSTIA bearing dataset, and the IMS bearing dataset would strengthen the generalizability of the findings.

The codebase for the QASAMAP framework, including the T-GCN implementation (\texttt{tcgn\_updated.py}), the agentic AI pipeline (\texttt{agenticai.py}), and the quantum-augmented pipeline (\texttt{quantum\_agent.py}), is available for research replication and extension. Researchers wishing to reproduce the experimental results or extend the framework should ensure the following dependencies: Python 3.8+, PyTorch 1.10+, Qiskit 0.45+, and access to the Ollama Cloud API for LLM agent orchestration. The synthetic dataset generation procedure is documented in the code, enabling creation of datasets with matching statistical properties for comparative studies.

\subsection{Future Research Directions}

The most immediately impactful future direction is the evaluation of the QASAMAP Layer~3 scheduling QUBO on physical quantum annealing hardware. D-Wave Advantage with its native Pegasus-topology Ising hardware is the most directly applicable platform; access via the D-Wave Leap programme would enable testing whether real-hardware quantum annealing can reach feasibility on the dense scheduling QUBO encoding where simulator-only QA\_neal consistently failed. Concurrently, alternative QUBO encodings (e.g., one-hot per machine without explicit employee dimension followed by post-processing, or constraint-relaxed reformulations amenable to D-Wave's native graph topology) may improve quantum-class solver behaviour. For gate-model QAOA, fault-tolerant or far-larger-scale NISQ hardware (e.g., IBM Heron 156-qubit, Quantinuum H2 56-qubit) would be required to handle the $\geq 45$ qubits demanded by even the smallest meaningful QASAMAP scheduling instance.

The second high-priority direction is the large-scale cross-validated evaluation of the full QASAMAP pipeline over the complete 100,000-observation dataset, replacing the scenario-based pipeline demonstration with rigorous statistical benchmarking over held-out test periods. This would provide statistical confidence intervals on all reported KPI metrics and enable direct comparison with the supervised learning baselines established in the classification and regression experiments.

The third direction is the extension of the multi-agent agentic layer to support enhanced LLM-based reasoning capabilities, including in-context learning from historical decision logs, chain-of-thought diagnostic reasoning over multi-step sensor degradation trajectories, and autonomous generation of detailed maintenance procedure reports. This would enhance the practical utility of the Reporting Agent output and reduce the domain expertise required to interpret and act on the system's recommendations.

The fourth direction is the investigation of federated learning extensions that enable multiple manufacturing facilities to collaboratively train shared predictive maintenance models without sharing raw sensor data. The multi-agent architecture of QASAMAP is naturally compatible with federated paradigms, and cross-facility collaboration would substantially increase the diversity and volume of training data available for rare failure mode detection.

\subsection{Broader Implications}

The research contributions of this thesis have implications extending beyond smart manufacturing predictive maintenance. The 3-layer QASAMAP architecture --- T-GCN spatiotemporal forecasting (Layer~1), multi-LLM cloud-ensemble agentic detection (Layer~2), and classical-or-quantum scheduling optimisation (Layer~3) --- provides a concrete template for transitioning AI-augmented predictive maintenance from research prototype to production deployment. The honest empirical reporting demonstrates that responsible academic practice can coexist with practical-deployment relevance: the V3 substantial-agreement Layer~2 result clears the threshold for downstream scheduling, while the Layer~3 quantum negative result is reported transparently rather than buried, allowing future researchers to focus on alternative QUBO encodings and real-hardware testing rather than re-discovering the same simulator-only limitation.

The demonstration that a 3-LLM cloud ensemble can autonomously assign multi-tier maintenance criticality with substantial agreement against composite ground truth, perfect Critical-or-High coverage of true Critical machines, and end-to-end real-time latency within the 60-s Kafka producer cycle illustrates a practical model for human-AI collaboration in industrial operations. Rather than positioning AI as a passive analytical tool, the QASAMAP framework establishes a model of active AI agency in which automated agents handle continuous monitoring and tier assignment while human supervisors retain oversight authority over the resulting maintenance schedule. The methodology evolution across three pre-registered deviations also illustrates a defensible iteration practice: each deviation documented with empirical justification, the same primary metric and threshold preserved throughout, and all intermediate results preserved transparently in the repository.

\subsection{Closing Remarks}

This thesis has demonstrated that the 3-layer QASAMAP architecture --- T-GCN spatiotemporal forecasting (Layer~1), multi-LLM cloud-ensemble agentic detection (Layer~2), and classical-versus-quantum scheduling optimisation (Layer~3) --- is both technically feasible and empirically defensible. Layer~2 achieves substantial agreement with composite ground truth ($\kappa = 0.7425$, 95\% CI [0.62, 0.83]) on 125 multi-window QASAMAP test cases. Layer~3 demonstrates that classical heuristics dominate quantum-class methods on this dense scheduling QUBO at simulator-only evaluation, with all four classical solvers converging to optimal makespan and sustaining real-time feasibility within the 60-s Kafka producer cycle. The end-to-end Layer~2 + Layer~3 pipeline operates within real-time budget by $\geq 26$~s margin. The honest empirical findings --- the substantial-agreement Layer~2 result, the classical-dominant Layer~3 result, and the explicit non-availability of real D-Wave hardware --- are reported transparently with three pre-registered methodology deviations documented inline. The QASAMAP framework establishes a deployable architecture and a defensible empirical evidence base; as real D-Wave hardware access becomes available and as gate-model NISQ scales beyond current $\sim$18-qubit practical simulator limits, the architectural principles demonstrated here provide a productive foundation for advancing quantum-augmented intelligent manufacturing while maintaining the academic integrity standards (pre-registration, halt rules, transparent deviation documentation) that this thesis exemplifies.
