\section{Foundations of Smart Manufacturing and Industry 4.0}

\input{figures/industry40_evolution}

\subsection{Evolution of Manufacturing Paradigms}

The transition from traditional manufacturing to smart manufacturing represents one of the most significant industrial transformations of the twenty-first century \cite{mobley_introduction_2002, jardine_review_2006}. As established in Chapter~1, Industry~4.0 integrates cyber-physical systems, the Industrial Internet of Things, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence to create autonomous, self-optimising production environments. This section reviews the literature spanning the foundational technologies that enable such environments, the anomaly detection and predictive maintenance methods applied within them, the emerging roles of quantum computing and agentic AI, and the comparative state of existing integrated approaches.

\subsection{Cyber-Physical Systems and Industrial IoT}

Smart manufacturing facilities are instrumented with dense networks of sensors that continuously stream multivariate time-series data encompassing temperature, vibration, humidity, pressure, and energy consumption. The empirical dataset analysed in this thesis, comprising 100,000 sensor readings from 50 machines over 69 days, exemplifies the data volumes and heterogeneity characteristic of real deployments. Beyond volume, these environments exhibit non-stationary distributions that drift with operational context, heterogeneous modalities with disparate scales, and strict latency requirements for autonomous decision-making \cite{zhang_unsupervised_2021}. Jain et al. \cite{jain_anomaly_2016} investigate anomaly detection in smart-city wireless sensor networks, demonstrating that spatial correlation between co-located sensors and robust handling of sensor failures versus genuine anomalies provide methodological insights directly transferable to the 50-machine industrial fleet studied herein.

\subsection{Data-Driven Maintenance Intelligence}

The convergence of big data analytics and industrial automation has enabled a shift from reactive to predictive maintenance paradigms. Gama et al. \cite{Gama2014} establish that data stream mining techniques are essential for adapting to evolving industrial environments where concept drift is inevitable. Zhao et al. \cite{Zhao2019} survey deep learning applications in smart manufacturing, identifying predictive maintenance as one of the highest-impact domains for neural network deployment. These foundational works collectively establish that modern smart manufacturing requires integrated systems capable of ingesting high-velocity sensor streams, fusing heterogeneous data modalities, and autonomously adapting to non-stationary operational conditions.

\section{Anomaly Detection in Manufacturing}

\input{figures/anomaly_taxonomy}

\subsection{Deep Learning-Based Anomaly Detection}

Anomaly detection in industrial time-series data has been revolutionised by deep learning architectures. Chalapathy and Chawla \cite{Chalapathy2019} provide the most comprehensive survey of deep anomaly detection, systematically reviewing autoencoders, variational autoencoders, recurrent neural networks, convolutional networks, and generative adversarial networks across application domains spanning cyber-intrusion detection, fraud, medical imaging, and industrial monitoring. Their taxonomy distinguishes unsupervised, semi-supervised, and supervised paradigms, demonstrating that reconstruction-based autoencoders trained exclusively on normal data are among the most effective approaches for industrial scenarios characterised by abundant normal data and rare anomalous events. Pang et al. \cite{pang_deep_2021} extend this foundation with a systematic review of deep learning for anomaly detection, confirming that normal-data-only training yields superior performance in imbalanced industrial settings. The 8.92\% anomaly rate in the research dataset aligns precisely with this scenario.

\subsection{Autoencoder and Memory-Augmented Architectures}

Standard autoencoders suffer from a critical weakness: they generalise so effectively during training that anomalous inputs can be reconstructed with low error, undermining the detection principle. Gao et al. \cite{Gao2022} address this limitation through TSMAE, a Memory-Augmented Autoencoder for IoT time-series data that stores prototypical normal patterns in a fixed-size memory module. The decoder reconstructs inputs solely from memory-retrieved representations, ensuring that anomalous inputs which do not match any stored prototype generate high reconstruction errors. This mechanism directly addresses the false-negative problem on the research dataset, where five distinct failure modes create multiple normality-deviation patterns that standard autoencoders might learn to reconstruct. Chen et al. \cite{chen_unsupervised_2020} extend the autoencoder paradigm with sliding-window convolutional variational autoencoders for industrial robots, demonstrating that converting continuous time series into fixed-length segments achieves state-of-the-art detection performance on robotic actuator degradation data.

\subsection{Multi-Sensor and Correlation-Based Detection}

Industrial machines are monitored through multiple heterogeneous sensors whose joint behaviour encodes richer failure signatures than any single modality. Zhang et al. \cite{zhang_unsupervised_2021} investigate unsupervised deep anomaly detection for multi-sensor time-series signals, demonstrating that architectures capable of modelling inter-sensor correlations substantially outperform single-sensor approaches. Han et al. \cite{Han2022} specifically examine correlation-based anomaly detection, showing that anomalous states can be reliably detected by monitoring deviations from learned normal cross-sensor correlation structures. On the research dataset, the correlation matrix reveals that temperature is the dominant anomaly predictor ($r = 0.447$), while disruptions to inter-sensor relationships provide early warning signals for impending failures. These findings provide theoretical justification for the multi-sensor fusion design in QASAMAP's encoding layer.

\subsection{Real-Time Anomaly Detection for Industrial IoT}

The specific requirements of real-time anomaly detection in industrial IoT settings include low-latency inference, resource-constrained edge deployment, and handling of streaming data with adaptive thresholds. Nizam et al. \cite{nizam_real-time_2022} propose a lightweight deep anomaly detection framework that combines LSTM-based detection with adaptive threshold adjustment based on operational context, aligning with the one-minute sensor sampling interval of the research dataset. Wen and Keyes \cite{wen_time_2019} advance CNN-based segmentation approaches that outperform RNN-based methods on several benchmarks while achieving substantially lower computational cost, with transfer learning components enabling pre-training on source-domain time series before fine-tuning on target industrial data. Chouhan et al. \cite{Chouhan2019} investigate network-level deep convolutional anomaly detection with residual learning, proposing channel-boosting mechanisms with multiple parallel branches and kernel sizes of 3, 5, and 7 that directly parallel the multi-scale temporal pattern capture required in manufacturing environments.

\section{Predictive Maintenance Approaches}

\input{figures/pdm_pipeline}

\subsection{Classical and Statistical Prognostics}

Traditional predictive maintenance relies on statistical process control, survival analysis, and physical degradation models. Mobley \cite{mobley_introduction_2002} introduces the foundational concepts of vibration analysis, oil analysis, and thermography for machinery diagnostics. Jardine et al. \cite{jardine_review_2006} provide a comprehensive review of diagnostics and prognostics implementing condition-based maintenance, covering statistical pattern recognition, hidden Markov models, and particle filtering for remaining useful life estimation. While these classical methods offer interpretability and well-understood failure thresholds, they require extensive domain expertise, manual feature engineering, and struggle to model the complex nonlinear relationships between high-dimensional sensor streams and degradation trajectories in modern smart manufacturing environments.

\subsection{Deep Learning for Remaining Useful Life Estimation}

Deep learning has emerged as the dominant paradigm for data-driven prognostics. Hochreiter and Schmidhuber \cite{Hochreiter1997} introduced long short-term memory networks, which remain the baseline recurrent architecture for temporal degradation modelling. The research dataset's near-uniform RUL distribution (mean 234.27 hours, standard deviation 150.06 hours) confirms that single-snapshot static-feature-based RUL prediction is fundamentally limited, motivating the use of sequential architectures. Ruff et al. \cite{Ruff2021} survey deep one-class classification methods that learn compact descriptions of normality, providing a theoretical framework for understanding how deep networks model the boundary between normal and degraded operational regimes. These works collectively establish that temporal deep learning is essential for capturing the sequential degradation trajectories required for accurate remaining useful life estimation.

\subsection{Multi-Task Learning for Joint Anomaly-RUL Prediction}

Despite substantial progress in both anomaly detection and remaining useful life estimation, the literature predominantly treats these objectives as isolated problems addressed by distinct research communities. Anomaly detection studies focus on identifying outlier patterns \cite{chen_unsupervised_2020}, while prognostics research concentrates on estimating remaining useful life from degradation trajectories \cite{nizam_real-time_2022}, with limited cross-fertilisation between these complementary tasks. Kendall et al. \cite{kendall_multi-task_2017} demonstrated that jointly learned representations can improve individual task performance through uncertainty-weighted loss balancing, yet the predictive maintenance literature has not systematically exploited multi-task principles. The QASAMAP framework addresses this gap by integrating anomaly detection, RUL prediction, failure type classification, and maintenance scheduling within a unified architecture where shared representations benefit all downstream tasks.

\subsection{Transfer Learning and Domain Adaptation}

Transfer learning enables models pre-trained on publicly available datasets to be fine-tuned on target industrial data with limited labelled examples. Wen and Keyes \cite{wen_time_2019} demonstrate that CNN-based transfer learning improves generalisation on minority failure classes with scarce training data. Lee et al. \cite{lee_autoencoder-based_2024} investigate autoencoder-based frameworks for distinguishing process anomalies from sensor failures, a distinction critically important in real deployments where sensor drift must be separated from genuine equipment degradation. These domain-adaptation techniques are particularly valuable for the research dataset's minority failure modes, where Electrical Fault constitutes only 1.01\% of readings.

\section{Agent-Based Systems and Agentic AI in Manufacturing}

\input{figures/agentic_architecture_lit}

\subsection{From Multi-Agent Systems to Agentic AI}

The concept of autonomous agents --- computational entities that perceive environments, maintain world models, reason about goals, and execute purposeful actions --- has been central to artificial intelligence research since its inception. Wooldridge \cite{Wooldridge1995} established the foundational theoretical framework for multi-agent systems, defining autonomy, reactivity, pro-activeness, and social ability as core agent properties. Sapkota et al. \cite{sapkota_ai_2026} provide the most current conceptual taxonomy, rigorously distinguishing between individual AI agents (discrete, task-specific autonomous entities) and agentic AI systems (integrated architectures in which multiple agents collaborate, plan, reason, and execute over extended horizons). This distinction is fundamental to QASAMAP: the nine individual agents are specialised LLM-powered modules, while the complete system constitutes an agentic AI architecture in the full sense defined by Sapkota et al.

\subsection{LLM-Powered Agent Architectures}

The rise of large language models has fundamentally altered the applicability of agentic AI to high-stakes industrial environments. Erukude et al. \cite{erukude_agentic_2025} document the emergence of LLM-powered autonomous agents, establishing the LLM reasoning core as the defining architectural innovation of contemporary agentic systems. Dwivedi et al. \cite{dwivedi_agentic_2026} clarify common misconceptions about agentic AI, identifying four key distinguishing properties: autonomous goal-directed behaviour, persistent memory across interaction cycles, dynamic tool invocation, and self-reflection capabilities. QASAMAP implements all four: the Planning Agent pursues fleet health optimisation autonomously; the ContextMemoryStore maintains continuously updated state; the orchestrator invokes deep learning inference, heuristic scheduling, and work-order generation dynamically; and the Self-Reflection Agent evaluates decision outcomes for continuous improvement.

\subsection{Agentic AI for Industrial Decision Support}

Abou Ali et al. \cite{AbouAli2026} survey agentic AI architectures, identifying five key components --- perception modules, world models, reasoning engines, action executors, and memory systems --- that characterise state-of-the-art systems. QASAMAP's agentic layer implements all five: the Monitoring and Diagnosis Agents serve as perception modules, the LLM reasoning chain provides the reasoning engine, the Scheduling Agent and action layer constitute the action executor, and the ContextMemoryStore with sliding window and decision log implements the memory system. Derouiche et al. \cite{derouiche_agentic_2025} provide protocol-level specifications for inter-agent communication and task delegation that inform QASAMAP's sequential pipeline design, where each agent's structured output feeds into the next agent's prompt context.

\subsection{Sensor Data Intelligence with Foundation Models}

The application of large language models to sensor data understanding represents an emerging frontier directly relevant to interpretable predictive maintenance. Ouyang et al. \cite{ouyang_llmsense_2024} demonstrate that LLMs can perform sophisticated causal reasoning about sensor data patterns when provided with structured natural language representations of sensor traces, enabling interpretation of why a temperature spike is anomalous given concurrent neighbouring-sensor readings and maintenance history. Xing et al. \cite{xing_deepsqa_2021} introduce DeepSQA, a framework for answering natural language questions about sensor streams, providing a template for human-agent interaction interfaces where maintenance engineers query machine health in conversational terms. Singh et al. \cite{singh_agentic_2025} survey agentic retrieval-augmented generation, showing that consulting historical maintenance records and equipment manuals substantially improves the relevance and accuracy of agent-generated recommendations compared to parametric knowledge alone.

\section{Quantum Computing and Quantum-Inspired AI for Manufacturing}

\input{figures/quantum_landscape}

\subsection{Quantum Optimization Foundations}

Quantum computing represents not merely an incremental improvement over classical computation but a fundamentally new computational paradigm requiring algorithmic design from first principles \cite{eldh_quantum_2025}. Preskill \cite{Preskill2018} introduced the NISQ (Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum) era concept, characterising current quantum processors with 50--1,000 qubits and finite gate fidelities. Blekos et al. \cite{Blekos2024} systematically analyse the complexity classes for which quantum optimization advantages are theoretically supported, identifying the barren plateau problem as the primary training challenge for variational quantum algorithms and reviewing mitigation strategies including layerwise training and warm-starting from classical solutions. These theoretical foundations inform the design of QASAMAP's quantum-inspired heuristics, which preserve the mathematical structure of quantum formulations while executing on classical hardware.

\subsection{Quantum Annealing and QUBO Formulations}

Quantum annealing solves combinatorial optimization problems by mapping them to Quadratic Unconstrained Binary Optimization formulations and finding minimum-energy states of an Ising Hamiltonian. Dalyac et al. \cite{dalyac_qualifying_2021} qualify quantum approaches for hard industrial optimization problems, demonstrating that quantum annealing provides competitive solution quality for practical problem sizes. Tambunan et al. \cite{Tambunan2022} provide detailed QUBO formulations for routing problems with weighted edge segments, showing that quantum annealers achieve solution quality within 5\% of optimal for up to 20 nodes. Holliday et al. \cite{holliday_advanced_2025} introduce constraint encoding techniques for time-window constraints in quantum annealing formulations directly applicable to maintenance scheduling with shift-based windows. Bova et al. \cite{Bova2021} review commercial quantum computing applications, identifying maintenance scheduling as one of the three highest-value near-term application areas for quantum optimization in industry.

\subsection{QAOA for Combinatorial Scheduling}

The Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm, introduced by Farhi et al. \cite{Farhi2014}, provides a gate-based approach to combinatorial optimization applicable to scheduling problems. Rosendo et al. \cite{rosendo_quantum_2025} demonstrate that QAOA achieves competitive solution quality compared to classical heuristics on vehicle routing problems of practical size on current NISQ hardware. Windgatter et al. \cite{windgatter_quantum_2025} explore software frameworks for mapping high-level optimization problems to quantum circuits, with problem-decomposition capabilities that partition large problems into hardware-compatible subproblems directly analogous to QASAMAP's hierarchical fleet decomposition. Dalal et al. \cite{dalal_digitized_2024} introduce digitised counterdiabatic QAOA variants that accelerate convergence at low circuit depths, valuable for NISQ hardware where coherence time limits circuit depth. Symons et al. \cite{symons_practitioners_2023} provide a practitioner's guide recommending QAOA for scheduling problems with quadratic objectives and linear constraints, directly validating the QASAMAP scheduling formulation.

\subsection{Hybrid Quantum-Classical Systems}

Hybrid quantum-classical architectures combine quantum subroutines with classical preprocessing and postprocessing to mitigate NISQ-era limitations. Kreplin et al. \cite{kreplin_squlearn_2025} develop the sQUlearn library for quantum machine learning, offering hardware-efficient ansatz designs and automatic gradient computation via the parameter-shift rule. Nagy et al. \cite{nagy_hybrid_2025} demonstrate that encoding classical observations in quantum latent spaces enables reinforcement learning agents to discover policies that classical agents cannot find within practical training budgets. Rodr\'iguez-Soriano et al. \cite{rodriguez-soriano_predicting_2025} address temporal variability in quantum processor error rates, motivating adaptive scheduling strategies that defer quantum computation to low-noise time windows. Epping et al. \cite{Epping2025} provide a systematic introduction to quantum software development and error mitigation. These hybrid paradigms directly inform QASAMAP's design, in which quantum-inspired classical heuristics preserve the mathematical structure of quantum formulations for future hardware migration.

\subsection{Integration of Quantum and Agentic Paradigms}

Despite substantial independent progress in quantum computing and agentic AI, the integration of these paradigms within a unified predictive maintenance framework remains absent from the literature. Existing quantum optimization works \cite{rosendo_quantum_2025, holliday_advanced_2025} focus on scheduling in isolation without integrating anomaly detection or remaining useful life signals. Conversely, agentic AI surveys \cite{sapkota_ai_2026, AbouAli2026} discuss multi-agent orchestration for general decision-making but do not incorporate quantum-inspired heuristics for combinatorial optimization. The QASAMAP framework closes this gap by feeding anomaly scores, RUL predictions, and failure type classifications from the deep learning layer into both the quantum-inspired heuristic layer for maintenance prioritization and the agentic layer for autonomous decision orchestration.

\subsection{Closing the Gap: Unified Frameworks}

The most significant limitation of existing approaches is the absence of integrated frameworks that simultaneously achieve multi-sensor anomaly detection, remaining useful life prediction, maintenance scheduling optimization, and autonomous agentic decision-making within a single closed-loop architecture. Current works address one or two of these objectives in isolation: anomaly detection literature \cite{Chalapathy2019, pang_deep_2021} treats detection as a terminal task rather than the first stage of a full predictive maintenance workflow; prognostics research \cite{nizam_real-time_2022} estimates RUL without linking predictions to actionable scheduling decisions; quantum optimization studies \cite{dalyac_qualifying_2021, Blekos2024} formulate abstract scheduling problems without grounding them in real sensor data or agentic action execution; and agentic AI works \cite{erukude_agentic_2025, dwivedi_agentic_2026} reason about decisions without integrating physics-informed quantum heuristics for combinatorial optimization. QASAMAP addresses this fragmentation by unifying spatiotemporal forecasting, multi-task supervised learning, quantum-inspired classical heuristics, and multi-agent LLM orchestration within a single architecture that autonomously adapts to evolving industrial conditions.

\subsection{Limitations of Current Methodologies}

A systematic comparison of state-of-the-art methodologies reveals three persistent gaps that constrain practical deployment. First, the siloed treatment of anomaly detection and prognostics prevents exploitation of shared representations that could improve both tasks simultaneously \cite{kendall_multi-task_2017}. Second, static model architectures cannot adapt to the non-stationary distributions inherent in real industrial operations \cite{lee_autoencoder-based_2024}. Third, the combinatorial complexity of scheduling maintenance across large machine pools exceeds the capabilities of classical greedy heuristics \cite{dalyac_qualifying_2021}. While individual works excel within their respective domains --- TSMAE for memory-augmented anomaly detection \cite{Gao2022}, QAOA for quantum scheduling \cite{rosendo_quantum_2025}, and agentic RAG for knowledge-augmented reasoning \cite{singh_agentic_2025} --- none provides an integrated solution that combines all three capabilities within a unified, adaptive, closed-loop architecture.

\subsection{Positioning of QASAMAP}

QASAMAP is positioned at the intersection of four active research fronts: spatiotemporal deep learning for industrial forecasting, multi-task supervised learning for joint health prediction, quantum-inspired classical heuristics for maintenance optimization, and agentic AI for autonomous decision orchestration. The framework integrates these components through a unified pipeline in which the T-GCN forecasting layer provides temporal context for the supervised classification and regression layer, whose outputs feed both the quantum-inspired heuristic layer for priority ranking and the agentic layer for decision-making. The ContextMemoryStore maintains state across cycles, the LearningLoop adapts thresholds from feedback, and the heuristic override mechanism guards against false negatives that classical thresholds alone would miss. This integration distinguishes QASAMAP from prior works that address individual aspects of predictive maintenance in isolation, advancing the state of the art toward fully autonomous, self-improving intelligent manufacturing maintenance systems.
