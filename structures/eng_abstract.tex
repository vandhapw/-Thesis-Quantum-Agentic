The emergence of Industry 4.0 has transformed manufacturing into cyber-physical systems where unplanned downtime carries severe economic consequences. Despite considerable progress, predictive maintenance research remains fragmented: anomaly detection, remaining useful life estimation, and maintenance scheduling are treated as isolated problems; static models degrade under sensor drift; and combinatorial scheduling complexity grows exponentially with fleet size. This thesis proposes the Quantum-Agentic Smart Manufacturing Anomaly detection and Predictive Maintenance (QASAMAP) framework, a three-layer integrated architecture designed to address these gaps within a single closed-loop system.

The first layer implements spatiotemporal forecasting and supervised learning on a 100,000-reading dataset collected from 50 machines over 69 days. A Temporal Graph Convolutional Network (T-GCN) with cross-correlation lag graph construction (50 nodes, 538 edges) achieves an average R\textsuperscript{2} of 0.7702 across five sensor channels, outperforming both standalone LSTM (0.6853, +12.4\%) and Hybrid LSTM-GCN (0.7134, +8.0\%) baselines. A comprehensive benchmark of five supervised algorithms across six prediction targets establishes baselines of F1 = 0.999 for anomaly detection, F1 = 0.880 for failure type classification, F1 = 0.872 for maintenance prediction, and R\textsuperscript{2} = 0.189 for remaining useful life regression.

The second layer deploys a multi-agent agentic AI system comprising nine LLM-powered agents coordinated by the \texttt{MultiAgentOrchestrator} via the Ollama Cloud API. The system autonomously manages the complete predictive maintenance decision cycle from sensor ingestion through maintenance dispatch, integrating contextual memory, heuristic override arbitration, and continuous learning. Post-audit honest evaluation reports F1 = 0.00 on a held-out 25-machine test split and F1 = 0.33 on the full 50-machine dataset (precision = 1.00, recall = 0.20), with an average prediction latency of 5,771.8~seconds. An LLM benchmark across 270 calls reveals JSON parse rates of 66.7--82.2\% and reasoning quality of 71.7--84.8\%.

The third layer augments the pipeline with quantum-\emph{inspired} classical heuristics that preserve the mathematical structure of their quantum counterparts for future hardware migration: Pauli-Z multi-sensor risk encoding, QUBO-formulated local search for priority ranking, and QAOA-formulated graph scheduling for maintenance slot assignment. A heuristic override mechanism escalates machines carrying danger signals to active inspection even when classical risk scores indicate LOW priority. In the experimental evaluation, this mechanism triggers actionable decisions for 5 out of 7 machines that classical thresholds would passively monitor. \textbf{Algorithmic disclosure:} All ``quantum'' components are implemented as classical heuristics on standard CPUs; no quantum processor is used. The experimental results therefore reflect classical heuristic performance.

By unifying spatiotemporal forecasting, multi-task supervised learning, quantum-inspired heuristic optimization, and agentic orchestration within a single architecture, QASAMAP advances intelligent manufacturing maintenance while providing an honest, reproducible benchmark for future research.
